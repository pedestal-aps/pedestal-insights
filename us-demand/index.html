The State of US Demand · 2026

The state of US demand is strong. On 23 September 2026, we arrived.

For more than two years, US visitors found Pedestal without a US storefront, dollar prices, US delivery or a single paid US ad, almost entirely organically. On 23 September 2026 the US store opened at us.pedestal.com, with prices in USD and delivery from a US warehouse. This is the state of that demand: how large, how concentrated, and how fast it is compounding, measured across our 11 European storefronts and, from launch day, the US store, with bot and AI-agent traffic stripped out so every number reflects a real person.

Where we stand

  • The US was a major market before we had a store. 182,838 real US visitor sessions reached our European storefronts in the 27 months before launch (Jun 2024 to Aug 2026), with EUR prices and no way to get the product delivered. They couldn't buy, and they kept coming.
  • Launch changed the slope. October 2026, the first full month with the US store open, brought 19,508 real US sessions: 9,093 on us.pedestal.com and 10,415 on the European storefronts. That is roughly double the 8,900 to 10,400 a month of June to August.
  • Demand is compounding, not drifting. Real US sessions grew +86% year over year, about 125,000 in the last 12 months (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026), up from about 67,200 in the prior 12, with a decisive step-up from October 2025. A log-linear trend across all 29 months implies ≈71% a year.
  • It is geographically concentrated. California, New York and Texas are about 40% of the total; the metro map centers on New York, Los Angeles, the Bay Area, Chicago, Seattle, Dallas and Atlanta, a tight, addressable footprint.
  • The barriers are gone. Until launch, a US visitor landed on EUR prices with no way to get the product delivered. Now the same visitor sees USD prices and gets delivery from a US warehouse, so traffic that couldn't convert before can turn into orders.
  • And we already have a lever that works. A light-touch program of just five gifted US creators (from October 2025) coincided with a marked, durable step-up in US sessions, an early signal that a small, deliberate push moves this market fast.

Methodology & data integrity

Source: Shopify "Sessions by location", United States only, monthly, Jun 2024 to Oct 2026. The 11 European storefronts are counted for the whole period, and the US store (us.pedestal.com) from its launch on 23 September 2026. US visitors still reach the European storefronts after launch, so both are counted to keep the series comparable.

Bots & AI agents flagged out. ~49% of raw "US sessions" came from cloud-datacenter locations such as Council Bluffs IA (Google), Ashburn VA (AWS), North Bergen NJ, Forest City NC (Meta) and Prineville OR. These are search/AI crawlers, on-demand AI fetchers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI) and monitoring bots; they geolocate to the server, not a person, so they're excluded from the real-customer view shown here. The full all-traffic figures (state, city) are included for completeness, and the chart above shows the split. Real humans who arrive from an AI answer (a person clicking through) are counted normally, at their real location.

Filter maintained as bot infrastructure moves. During 2026 the older bot hubs went quiet and new ones ramped up, mostly Meta's newer datacenter sites. The exclusion list was extended in August 2026 (Social Circle GA, Gallatin TN, DeKalb IL, Huntsville AL and Eagle Mountain UT, all Meta, plus Hillsboro OR, St Louis MO, Columbus OH, Springfield NE and Sandston VA) and re-applied to the full series, so every month is stated on the same basis. This trimmed the previously published history by ~3%. In November 2026 two more datacenter towns were added (Eatonton GA, and Los Lunas NM, a Meta site), and bot bursts were removed from two real metros, San Jose CA (Aug to Oct 2026) and Kansas City MO (Sep 2026): in those months the extra traffic hit all 11 European storefronts evenly, the fingerprint of a datacenter rather than of people. The US store's sessions from both cities are kept. Earlier months moved by fewer than 20 sessions.

Growth is measured year over year, the most recent 12 months against the prior 12, to neutralize seasonality; a log-linear trend across all 29 months implies ≈71%/year.
